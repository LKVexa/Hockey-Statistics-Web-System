The engine

The engine is the part of RINK-VT that knows hockey: the game actor that folds frames into a game's state, the expected-goals model, the league-wide models, the heat maps and the HK001 laboratory. It is the same in both editions.

The game actor

The actor is a pure fold of the accepted frames of one game. Replaying a game's ledger must reach the identical state digest; this is tested in the reference edition and checked by rink ledger verify. Within the fold:

Clock latching
The scoreboard history is piecewise; each event takes the clock at its first keystroke, not at Enter. A late clock command takes effect at the newest moment the game knows, never before it, so the clock history stays ordered.
Rosters
A player is found by code, J## or jersey. Club quotas: at most three overage (20-year-old) and two import players dressed.
Locations
Explicit coordinates, then the zone's centroid, then the specification's prior (71, 0). A prior location gets no danger class.
Causal chaining
Assists bind to the team's open goal for 15 s of operator time, and the window stays open until the next primary event; a third assist is rejected. A faceoff gives the opposing centre the complementary result automatically.
Inferences
A rebound is an attempt within 3.0 s of the same team's attempt with no stoppage between. A rush is an attempt within 4.0 s of the same team's zone entry, zone exit, takeaway or odd-man rush, or of an opponent's giveaway.
Penalties
Minors, double minors, majors, misconducts and game misconducts. At most two serve at once and the rest queue; a power-play goal releases the earliest-ending releasable minor; strength changes at the exact expiry time. A game misconduct ejects the player for the game: later events, shifts and lineups for the player are refused, and undoing the penalty lifts it.
Plus/minus
Not awarded for power-play goals; empty-net goals count.
Game end
In regulation; in a 3-on-3 sudden-death overtime of 5:00; or, in the regular season, by shootout (best of three, then sudden death). Playoff overtime periods are 20:00 sudden death.
Corrections
A correction must leave its event valid, or it is refused and nothing changes ({{ref:corrections}}).
Late frames
A terminal that catches up sends frames typed in the past. Each event keeps the clock of its own keystroke, but never rewinds the stint timeline: a late lineup or faceoff rotates lines at the latest time the game has seen. A T that moves the clock back ends the current stint where play had reached and starts a new one; time played again is counted again.
Bounds
A game holds at most 20 000 accepted frames; a regular-season game has periods 1 to 5, other stages up to 12. A tracked team with fewer than three skaters listed falls back to the penalty count for its strength. The scoreboard's version only ever increases.

Expected goals and danger

Expected goals (xG) follow the specification's spatiotemporal log-odds model (its §5.1). On the canonical rink — 200 × 85 ft, centre ice at (0, 0), the acting team attacking the goal line at x = +89 with posts at (89, ±3) — an attempt at (x, y) has

dx = max(0.5, 89 − x)      d = √(dx² + y²)      θ = atan2(|y|, dx)
φ  = arccos((d_L² + d_R² − 36) / (2 · d_L · d_R))                    the open-net angle between the posts
logit = −1.35 − 0.048·d − 0.022·θ° + 0.031·φ° + 0.85·rebound + 0.55·rush + 1.10·royal road
        + 0.35·[tip or snap] − 0.25·[slap] + 0.28·[shooter's strength 5v4, 5v3 or 4v3]
xG = σ(logit), rounded to four decimal places

Within a flurry — attempts at most 3.0 s apart without a stoppage — each attempt is discounted by the chance that an earlier one already scored:

x̃G_1 = xG_1      x̃G_k = xG_k · Π_(j < k) (1 − xG_j)

An attempt without coordinates is placed at its zone's centroid, and without a zone at the specification's prior (71, 0):

Stints

A stint is a stretch of play with fixed personnel and state. Stints are sliced at every lineup, faceoff, goal, penalty start and expiry, and period change. Each records the home and away skaters on the ice, the zone start, a power-play-expiry flag (within 15 s), the score state, Corsi, Fenwick, shots and expected goals for, and time on ice at even strength, on the power play and short-handed. The stints are what RAPM regresses on.

The NHLe factors are placeholders, not fitted values. A league that reports NHLe MUST replace them with its own calibration, and SHOULD treat every model as descriptive of the games recorded, under these assumptions.

League-wide answers are cached until the league changes, and per-game facts are computed once per game state. The two model fits (RAPM and GAR) run in slices of about 10 ms. In the reference edition's measurements, a cold RAPM fit over 200 simulated games takes about 0.3 s and a repeat is immediate.

Heat maps

The rink is a 120 × 51 lattice of 1⅔-ft cells. Eight maps are computed at once — home and away × attempts, goals, high-danger attempts and shots on goal — by a box kernel applied k times (about 15 ft at k = 4) in integer arithmetic. The reference edition runs the kernel on its Plume XPU (WebAssembly) and checks that the XPU, a JavaScript kernel and the LCUF-A reference interpreter give identical integers; this edition runs the JavaScript kernel, which gives the same integers.

HK001

HK001 MSSL 2.3.0 is a deterministic synthetic research laboratory written in Go. The engine carries a port of it that reproduces the Go program exactly — including its floating-point operation order, its JSON encoding and its input adapter — so that a receipt computed in the workbench is the receipt the Go program computes.

  • {{n_codes}} computation codes, each a command: 109 hockey equations (OFF-001 … ADV-005); 32 Master-Law models and 276 traced intermediates; 26 X8S models and 67 intermediates; 26 8Sphere models and 17 intermediates; and 10 receipt summaries (HK-GATE … HK-SHA). Appendix E lists them.
  • {{n_inputs}} input codes, HKI-001 … HKI-109, each with its mnemonic, unit, scope, derivation class and the tokens that feed it (Appendix D).
  • The bridge fills all 109 inputs for a team over a game or the season from the ledger, recording each value's provenance:
ProvenanceWhere the value comes from

Fidelity. In the reference edition's tests, the demonstration receipt's canonical SHA-256 is faa8e35a… on both of Go's Exp code paths and the printed receipt is byte-identical to the Go program's; 2 540 bundled Go math vectors pass; and 568 input files recorded from HK001's own Go adapter and engine are replayed (accepted or refused, error text, parsed request, receipt). The comparisons are described in the reference edition's docs/RINK-VT.md.

Properties of HK001 that users meet. A small sample can make HK001 refuse a case: zero denominators (one game in progress) produce NaN, which Go's JSON encoder refuses, so the receipt is NO_RECEIPT while every code is still evaluated; a season scope gives receipts. HK001's lattice quantisation clamps each Master-Law coordinate to {−0.1, 0, +0.1}; it is ported as it is (case cal lattice_bound 10 gives a wider lattice). These are properties of the laboratory, kept for exactness, not faults of the port.
